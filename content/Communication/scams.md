
The first thing to be aware of is suspicious companies ordering our products. Usually they order one quantity and they are a lighting company or a furniture company and that's a telltale sign that they want to buy it so that they can rip it off. This happened previously with a lighting company which inquired and we kind of didn't really send them what they wanted. It just died off but there is a possibility in which we refuse to sell or say that something is out of stock for a suspicious customer 

I also had a case in which somebody posed as a furniture store in Portugal but was really a Russian, some sort of hacking or unscrupulous person. They had a scam in which they created a link that looked like a fairly widely used legitimate file-sharing site called vTransfer. They knocked off the site, created a knockoff of it, and asked us to enter our email and our Gmail password to access the files.

First of all if files are password-protected they will have a one-time use password, not an OTP but a password that's just for that particular set of files. They usually don't ask for your account password. Usually no, never ask for your account password and never type it anywhere else apart from Gmail. This can compromise the entire account. They can also be customers who can be very unscrupulous. We had this lady from Gujarat Palak Patel who wanted to purchase our handles. She had ordered samples to check them out and ordered products and kept asking for discounts but she never really let up on it. It took almost a year of talking to her for her to finally order and she kept the samples and never gave them back to us. She still has the samples and I don't want to term a certain state or anybody as more given to this sort of behavior than the others. In general just try to be as assertive as you can and do not give up more than one or two samples and only even share the samples when they are trustworthy because essentially we're working on trust.
